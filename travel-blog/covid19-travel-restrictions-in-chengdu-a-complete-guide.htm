COVID-19 Travel Restrictions in Chengdu: A Complete Guide

  • Home
  • /
  • Travel Blog
  • /
  • Blog Details

Chengdu, the capital of Sichuan province, has long been a magnet for travelers drawn to its spicy cuisine, giant pandas, and laid-back lifestyle. But since the pandemic reshaped global travel, navigating the city’s entry requirements has become a puzzle. This guide breaks down everything you need to know about COVID-19 travel restrictions in Chengdu as of late 2023, with practical tips on visas, testing, quarantine, and local hotspots that are still buzzing despite the new normal.

Understanding Chengdu’s Current Entry Policies

Chengdu, like all Chinese cities, follows the national framework set by the National Health Commission, but local implementation can vary. As of October 2023, China has lifted most of its strict zero-COVID measures, but some remnants remain for international arrivals. Here’s what you need to know before booking your flight to Chengdu Tianfu International Airport (TFU) or the older Shuangliu International Airport (CTU).

Visa and Pre-Departure Requirements

First, ensure your visa is valid. China resumed issuing most visa categories in early 2023, but tourist visas (L-visa) are still processed with caution. If you’re planning a leisure trip, apply at least four weeks in advance. For business travelers, the M-visa is easier to obtain, and many are using it to combine work with sightseeing.

Before departure, you must complete a health declaration form through the China Customs WeChat mini-program or the official website. This form asks about your vaccination status, recent travel history, and any symptoms. Print a copy or save a screenshot, as you’ll need to show it at check-in and upon arrival.

Testing and Vaccination Rules

China no longer requires a negative PCR test for entry, but airlines may still ask for a rapid antigen test taken within 48 hours before boarding. Check with your carrier—some, like Air China or Sichuan Airlines, are stricter than others. Vaccination is not mandatory for entry, but being fully vaccinated (with WHO-approved vaccines like Pfizer, Moderna, Sinovac, or Sinopharm) can speed up your clearance at customs. If you’re unvaccinated, you might face additional questioning, but no quarantine is required.

On-Arrival Procedures at Chengdu Airports

When you land, you’ll go through thermal screening and a health code check. The “健康码” (health code) is still used in China, but international travelers can now get a temporary code at the airport. Download the Alipay or WeChat app beforehand, as they have built-in health code features. After clearing immigration, you’ll collect your luggage and proceed to customs. There’s no mandatory quarantine for asymptomatic travelers, but if you show symptoms, you may be directed to a designated hotel for observation.

Navigating Chengdu’s Local COVID-19 Measures

Once you’re inside Chengdu, the city feels surprisingly normal. Masks are rarely seen outdoors, but they’re still required in hospitals, pharmacies, and on public transit. Most restaurants, bars, and attractions are open, but some venues may ask for a green health code on entry. Here’s how to handle day-to-day life.

Public Transportation and Ride-Hailing

Chengdu’s metro system is efficient and cheap. You can buy a single-ride ticket at machines, but it’s easier to use a transit card or Alipay’s transport feature. Buses also run frequently, and ride-hailing apps like Didi Chuxing are widely used. Drivers may ask you to wear a mask, so keep one handy. Taxis are available, but surge pricing is common during peak hours.

Dining and Nightlife: The Chengdu Way

Chengdu is famous for its food scene, and it’s thriving. Hotpot restaurants like Haidilao or local joints in Kuanzhai Alley are packed with locals. No proof of vaccination is needed to dine in, but some upscale venues might scan your health code. Street food stalls are also back in full swing—try the “麻辣兔头” (spicy rabbit head) or “担担面” (dandan noodles). Nightlife is lively, especially in the Lan Kwai Fong area or the bars along Jinli Street. Clubs and karaoke bars are open, but they may close earlier than pre-pandemic times.

Attractions and Tourist Sites

Major attractions are operating normally but with limited capacity. The Chengdu Research Base of Giant Panda Breeding is a must-visit, and you can book tickets online through their official WeChat account. Arrive early to see the pandas during feeding time. Other highlights include the Wuhou Shrine, Jinli Ancient Street, and the Leshan Giant Buddha (a day trip from Chengdu). Museums like the Sichuan Museum require advance reservations, and masks are mandatory inside.

Hot Topics and Travel Trends in Chengdu Post-COVID

The pandemic has reshaped how travelers experience Chengdu. Here are three trends that are defining the city’s tourism recovery.

The Rise of Domestic Tourism and “Staycations”

With international travel still recovering, Chinese tourists have flocked to Chengdu for domestic trips. This has led to overcrowding at popular spots during holidays like National Day (October 1-7) and Chinese New Year. If you’re visiting during these periods, book accommodations months in advance. Hotels in the Jinjiang District or near Chunxi Road are central but pricey. For a quieter stay, consider the outskirts like Dujiangyan, which offers pandas and ancient irrigation systems.

Digital Nomads and Remote Work in Chengdu

Chengdu has become a hub for digital nomads due to its low cost of living and fast internet. Coworking spaces like “Naked Hub” or “WeWork” are scattered across the city, and many cafes offer free Wi-Fi. The city’s relaxed vibe attracts freelancers from around the world. However, note that using VPNs is still restricted in China. Download a reliable VPN before arrival, as many (like ExpressVPN or NordVPN) are blocked. Some travelers use “shadow socks” or “V2Ray” as alternatives.

Health and Safety Concerns for Travelers

While COVID-19 is no longer a major threat, travelers should still take precautions. Pharmacies sell masks, hand sanitizer, and rapid test kits. If you develop symptoms, visit a hospital like West China Hospital, which has an international department. Travel insurance that covers COVID-19 treatment is strongly recommended, as medical costs can be high for foreigners. Also, keep digital copies of your passport, visa, and health documents in case of emergencies.

Practical Tips for a Smooth Trip to Chengdu

To avoid hiccups, follow these actionable steps.

Download Essential Apps Before You Go

WeChat and Alipay are non-negotiable. They handle payments, health codes, and even taxi bookings. Google services are blocked, so use Baidu Maps for navigation. For translation, Pleco is better than Google Translate. Also, install a VPN if you need access to Western social media or news.

Currency and Payments

China is largely cashless. Most vendors accept WeChat Pay or Alipay, but international credit cards are rarely accepted outside of luxury hotels. Bring some RMB for street vendors or small shops. ATMs at airports and banks accept foreign cards, but fees apply.

Language Barriers and Cultural Etiquette

English is not widely spoken outside of tourist areas. Learn a few phrases like “谢谢” (thank you) and “多少钱” (how much). Download a translation app for emergencies. In terms of etiquette, avoid discussing sensitive topics like politics or the pandemic. Locals are friendly but may be cautious around foreigners—smile and be patient.

The Future of Travel in Chengdu

Chengdu is positioning itself as a global travel destination, with new direct flights from cities like Los Angeles, London, and Tokyo. The city is also investing in eco-tourism, with projects like the Panda National Park and the renovation of ancient towns like Huanglongxi. As COVID-19 fades, expect more festivals and events to return, such as the Chengdu International Food Festival in September and the Lantern Festival in February.

However, travelers should stay flexible. Policies can change quickly, especially during outbreaks of new variants. Follow the official WeChat accounts of Chengdu’s tourism bureau and the Chinese Embassy in your home country for real-time updates.

Final Thoughts Before You Pack

Chengdu is more accessible now than it has been in years, but it’s not a free-for-all. The city rewards planners who do their homework. If you’re willing to navigate the health codes, app-based payments, and occasional mask mandates, you’ll discover a metropolis that blends ancient traditions with modern resilience. Whether you’re here for the pandas, the spicy hotpot, or the laid-back teahouses, Chengdu is ready to welcome you—just bring your patience and a fully charged phone.
